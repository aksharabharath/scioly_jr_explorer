## 5. `SOURCE-VERIFIED` — inspected USGS (7)

Use only the quoted claim. Do **not** import nearby numbers (hypoxia 2 mg/L, salinity ppm bands, “ten molecules per million”) into the MVP.

| ID | Claim (paraphrase for the matrix) | Quotation / evidence note |
|---|---|---|
| V-U1 | Dissolved oxygen is oxygen dissolved in water, available to aquatic organisms, and is used as a water-quality measure. | T2-USGS-DO: “Dissolved oxygen (DO) is a measure of how much oxygen is dissolved in the water - the amount of oxygen available to living aquatic organisms.” |
| V-U2 | Cold water can hold more dissolved oxygen than warm water (qualitative DO–temperature relationship). | T2-USGS-DO: “Cold water can hold more dissolved oxygen than warm water.” T2-USGS-TEMP: “Warm water holds less dissolved oxygen than cool water.” |
| V-U3 | Water temperature influences biological activity/growth and which organisms live in a water body. | T2-USGS-TEMP: “Temperature exerts a major influence on biological activity and growth. Temperature governs the kinds of organisms that can live in rivers and lakes.” |
| V-U4 | Nitrogen (including nitrate) is a plant nutrient; excess can overstimulate aquatic plants and algae. | T2-USGS-N: “Nitrogen, in the forms of nitrate, nitrite, or ammonium, is a nutrient needed for plant growth.” “Excess nitrogen can cause overstimulation of growth of aquatic plants and algae.” |
| V-U5 | Phosphorus is essential for plants; too much can speed eutrophication described as reduced dissolved oxygen from extra nutrients. | T2-USGS-P: “Phosphorus is … an essential element for plant life, but when there is too much of it in water, it can speed up eutrophication (a reduction in dissolved oxygen in water bodies caused by an increase of mineral and organic nutrients).” Rules say **phosphates**, not phosphorus — see HR7. |
| V-U6 | Saline water contains significant concentrations of dissolved salts (commonly sodium chloride). | T2-USGS-SAL: “Water that is saline contains significant amounts (referred to as ‘concentrations’) of dissolved salts, the most common being … sodium chloride (NaCl).” ppm classification bands **not** used for MVP. |
| V-U7 | Biochemical oxygen demand is oxygen consumed by microbes decomposing organic matter under aerobic conditions at a specified temperature; used as an organic-pollution index (often at wastewater plants). | T2-USGS-BOD: “Biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) represents the amount of oxygen consumed by bacteria and other microorganisms while they decompose organic matter under aerobic (oxygen is present) conditions at a specified temperature.” “BOD is used, often in wastewater-treatment plants, as an index of the degree of organic pollution in water.” |

**Not verified this pass:** pH definition; turbidity definition; field-meter **procedures**; BOD 5-day / 20 °C method details; any feeding habit; any specimen diagnostic.
