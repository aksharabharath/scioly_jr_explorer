## 0. How to read this document

### Scope vs factual evidence

The rules establish **what can be tested**. They do **not** automatically establish the factual answer to a science question.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `RULES-SCOPE` | Named as testable content, format, or skill. |
| `SOURCE-VERIFIED` | The rules themselves contain the answer (printed name, class cell, logistics sentence). |
| `SOURCE-NEEDED` | Students are expected to know or do this, but the fact/procedure is not in the rules. |
| `IMAGE-SOURCE-NEEDED` | Needs a chart, graph, table, photo, or dataset this repo does not have. |
| `HUMAN-REVIEW` | Official text is ambiguous or oddly spelled; do not “fix” it in a bank without a decision. |

Do **not** mark a biological, chemical, or environmental fact `SOURCE-VERIFIED` merely because the rules list it as an example topic (`such as`) or as something students should know (ecology, feeding habits, procedures).

### Knowledge vs skill

- **Knowledge:** a fact, name, class membership, or named topic students may need to know.
- **Skill:** something students must **do** (identify, interpret, classify from the official table, relate parameters).

A skill is listed only if the rules establish it or it is directly required by an explicit listed concept. Nouns in the `such as` ecology list are **knowledge-scope names**, not eleven extra skills.

1. **Ecology content** under the open `such as` list — need inspected freshwater / treatment / ecology sources.  
2. **Feeding habits and general ecology** for 24 + 8 listed names — organism references; plants `HUMAN-REVIEW`.

6. **Visual and data assets** for ID and for graphs/tables.  
7. **HUMAN-REVIEW** spellings and slash-names; whole-test MCQ; online data vs materials ban.

Preferred **kinds** of sources (not yet evidence): EPA, USGS, NOAA, CDC, peer-reviewed or standard reference texts, official event-provided materials. None of these are in-repo for Water Quality as of this pass.
