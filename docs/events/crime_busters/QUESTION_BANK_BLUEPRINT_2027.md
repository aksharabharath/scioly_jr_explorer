## 3. Slot types that **are** in MVP (still unwritten)

### 3.1 Fingerprint (~10–14)

**Allowed operations (not stems):**

- Which names are the three fundamental families (Loops, Whorls, Arches).
- Which **printed** subtypes sit under Loops / Whorls / Arches **as the 2027 rules list them** (Ulnar, Radial, Central Pocket under Loops).
- Ulnar vs radial from forearm bones / little-finger vs radius (E-CB-004).
- Loop: enter, recurve, same-side exit (E-CB-002 / E-CB-003).
- Plain arch vs tented arch (E-CB-005 / E-CB-006).
- Plain / double-loop / accidental **whorl** definitions (E-CB-007, E-CB-008, E-CB-011, E-CB-012) — **without** asking which family Central Pocket belongs to in FBI/NIST.
- Minutiae include ridge **endings** and **bifurcations** (E-CB-013 / E-CB-014).
- Epidermis and dermis as the two main skin layers; fingerprints form at stratum basale / dermal papillae (E-CB-016 / E-CB-017). Do **not** import A&P bank items (`HR3`).

**Not in MVP:** print matching (S1 / IM1); spotting minutiae on a figure (IM2); island/spur/enclosure catalogs (SN1); “parts of the skin” beyond the quoted OpenStax claims (SN2); any stem whose correct answer is “Central Pocket is a whorl” (`HR8`).

### 3.2 Hair (~3–5)

- The five official hair sources as a closed list.
- Shaft layers: cuticle, cortex, medulla (E-CB-019).
- Human vs non-human: medulla often &lt;⅓ vs often &gt;⅓; imbricate vs varied scales (E-CB-020 / E-CB-021).

**Not in MVP:** dog vs cat vs squirrel vs mouse keys (SN3); hair micrographs (IM5).

### 3.3 Fiber (~3–5)

- The six official fiber **names** and the three official **class words** (animal, vegetable, synthetic) as **list literacy**.
- Optional: cotton is largely cellulose / plant carbohydrate (E-CB-024) — do not treat that as a complete class key for all six fibers.
- Optional: nylon, rayon, polyester are FTC **manufactured** fibers (E-CB-023) — **do not** equate manufactured with synthetic in a keyed item until `HR-FIB` is resolved.

**Not in MVP:** micrograph ID (IM6); linen and silk class facts (SN4, SN5); a four-option “which class is rayon?” item.

### 3.4 Soil (~6–8)

- The six official type **names**.
- Qualitative RHS: clay heavy / wet-cold / baked dry; sand light / free-draining / low nutrients; silt moisture-retentive / easily compacted; loam mix avoiding extremes; peat high organic matter and moisture; chalky alkaline / contains calcium carbonate (E-CB-026–031).
- Optional feel language (gritty / sticky / soapy) as **text** (E-CB-032), not as a photo.

**Not in MVP:** sample or photo ID (IM7); USDA % sand/silt/clay (SN7); using contest powder reagents to ID soil; treating **Chalky** soil as the powder **Chalk**.

### 3.5 Chemical (~10–14)

- Eight candidate powder **names**; six liquid **names**; prepared for **3–5** powders and **2–4** liquids (counts only).
- Formulas: NaCl (table salt); C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ (sucrose / table sugar — `HR-SUGAR`: treat contest “Sugar” as sucrose only if the stem says sucrose/table sugar); NaHCO₃ (baking soda); CaCO₃ (calcium carbonate / chalk powder); H₂O; H₂O₂ (hydrogen peroxide; **3%** is the official qualifier, not a PubChem concentration proof).
- Uses that are quoted: baking soda in baking powder / CO₂ source; CaCO₃ as antacid. Do not invent a full household-use table (`HR5`).
- Iodine: starch → bluish black; not starch → yellowish brown (E-CB-041). **Do not** use iodine to uniquely ID flour vs cornstarch (SN14).
- HCl: carbonates and hydrogen carbonates evolve CO₂; CaCO₃ + 2 HCl equation (E-CB-039 / E-CB-040). **Do not** use HCl alone to uniquely ID baking soda vs chalk (SN15).
- Liquids: rubbing alcohol associated with isopropanol / ~70% aqueous as rubbing alcohol (E-CB-043); vinegar associated with acetic acid (E-CB-044); household bleach contains sodium hypochlorite (E-CB-045). Stem these as **identity**, not as a single pure-compound formula for the whole bottle (`HR6`).
- Allowed reagents **named**: iodine, HCl, distilled water — as a **rule fact**, not as a fake results table.

**Not in MVP:** flour / yeast / gelatin / cornstarch as a single formula (SN8–SN11); full unknown-powder flowchart (SN13, SN16, SN17); lemon-juice formula (SN19); **any numeric pH** (SN18, SN24); wet-lab photos (IM8).

### 3.6 Safety / optional logistics (0–3)

- Do not consume test powders/liquids.
- HCl on skin or in eyes: rinse **at least 15 minutes**.

**Not in MVP:** first-aid as a large block; cheat-sheet trivia as more than **one** optional item; Overall Analysis paragraph practice (`HR7`); chromatography (`HR2` / SN22); invented crime stories (SN21).

## 4. Explicit exclusions (do not author yet)

- Fingerprint, hair, fiber, or soil **identification from images or samples**
- Placeholder images, fake chromatograms, or synthetic unknown-powder data tables
- Species-level hair diagnostics
- Fiber class keys that resolve `HR-FIB` without a human decision
- pH numbers or EPA drinking-water 6.5–8.5 as contest “Water”
- Mixture identification protocols
- Overall Analysis / motive / suspect reconstruction
- Chromatography
- Importing Anatomy & Physiology bank facts for “parts of the skin”
- FBI/NIST **family** of Central Pocket as the keyed answer
- Logistics (team of 2, 45 min, 5% penalty) as more than **0–1** optional items
- Any claim still `SOURCE-NEEDED` in the matrix

## 7. Is 40–50 realistically supportable?

**Yes as a ceiling, not as a quota.**

- **~30–36** if the first bank stays strictly T1-RULES list literacy plus a few formulas.
- **~36–44** if ulnar/radial, arch/loop/whorl **defs**, human-vs-animal hair, RHS soil properties, starch–iodine, and carbonate–acid are used carefully.
- **50** only with extra **verified** list-distinction items, not new lab results or pH values.

A verified ~38 is better than an invented 45.

Known blockers that shrink the bank if authoring is strict: `HR8`, `HR-FIB`, `HR6`, SN13–SN18, IM1–IM8.
